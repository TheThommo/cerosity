# Golf Governing Bodies

## The R&A

The R&A, based in St Andrews, Scotland, governs golf worldwide outside the United States and Mexico. It jointly authors the Rules of Golf with the USGA and administers The Open Championship, the oldest of golf's four Major championships. The R&A also manages the World Amateur Golf Ranking and oversees equipment testing and approval through its Equipment Standards department.

Founded in 1754 as the Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews, the organisation separated its governance and championship functions from the private club in 2004. The R&A now serves over 40 million golfers across 145 countries through its affiliated national federations.

For athletes, understanding that the R&A sets the rules framework matters because rule knowledge reduces Red Head anxiety on the course. When you know the rules, you make faster, more confident decisions under pressure — a Blue Head advantage.

*Source: R&A official website (randa.org); Rules of Golf 2023 edition*

## The USGA

The United States Golf Association governs golf in the United States and Mexico. Co-author of the Rules of Golf with the R&A, the USGA conducts 14 national championships annually including the US Open, US Women's Open, and US Amateur. It also manages the USGA Handicap System (now part of the World Handicap System) and sets equipment and ball testing standards.

Founded in 1894, the USGA serves as the national federation for American golf and works closely with state and regional golf associations. Its research and testing centre in Far Hills, New Jersey, evaluates equipment conformity and course rating.

For athletes, the USGA's handicap system provides the objective measurement framework that enables fair competition across all skill levels — removing a potential source of Red Head frustration about fairness.

*Source: USGA official website (usga.org); USGA history archives*

## The International Golf Federation (IGF)

The International Golf Federation coordinates golf at the Olympic Games and manages the World Amateur Team Championships. Founded in 1958, the IGF includes over 150 member federations. Golf returned to the Olympics in 2016 at Rio de Janeiro after a 112-year absence.

The IGF works with the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) to implement anti-doping protocols for international amateur and Olympic golf competition.

*Source: IGF official website (igfgolf.org); International Olympic Committee*
