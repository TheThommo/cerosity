# Pace of Play

## The Policy Framework

Pace of play is governed by Rule 5.6 of the Rules of Golf, which requires players to play without unreasonable delay. Both the R&A and USGA publish pace of play guidelines, and most professional tours enforce specific time-per-shot policies with penalties for slow play.

On the PGA Tour, the standard allowance is 40 seconds for the first player to play and 50 seconds when it is the player's turn but they are first to play in the group. The DP World Tour and LPGA Tour operate similar timing policies. Penalties escalate from warnings to one-stroke penalties to disqualification.

## Mental Performance Connection

Slow play is both a governance issue and a mental performance issue. For the slow player, excessive deliberation often signals Red Head overthinking — the inability to commit to a decision. For playing partners, waiting creates frustration that triggers its own Red Head spiral.

Blue Head pace management means:
- Having a consistent pre-shot routine with a defined time limit
- Making club selection and shot decisions while walking to the ball, not after arriving
- Being ready to play when it is your turn
- Accepting your decision and committing to execution rather than re-deliberating

The best competitive golfers play decisively. Indecision is Red Head. Commitment — even to an imperfect plan — is Blue Head.

## Amateur Golf Context

Most amateur golf is played without official pace of play timing, but etiquette expectations apply. A standard round for a fourball should take approximately four hours. Courses increasingly use pace of play marshals and GPS-tracked cart systems to monitor group positions.

Slow play is the single most common complaint among amateur golfers and a significant barrier to growing the game.

*Source: R&A/USGA Rules of Golf 2023, Rule 5.6; PGA Tour pace of play policy; R&A pace of play manual*
