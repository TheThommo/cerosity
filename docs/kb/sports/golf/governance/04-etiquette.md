# Golf Etiquette and Code of Conduct

## The Spirit of the Game

Golf is unique among major sports in that players are expected to self-govern their conduct and often self-police rule violations. The Rules of Golf open with a section on "The Spirit of the Game" which establishes that all players are expected to play with integrity, show consideration for others, and take care of the course.

This self-governance tradition places a significant mental performance burden on players. Unlike team sports with referees making real-time calls, golfers must make honest rulings on themselves — sometimes at significant competitive cost.

## Core Etiquette Standards

Standard etiquette expectations in golf include:

**On the course:**
- Remain still and silent when another player is addressing the ball or making a stroke
- Do not stand in another player's line of sight or line of putt
- Be ready to play when it is your turn
- Repair pitch marks on greens and replace divots on fairways
- Rake bunkers after play

**Safety:**
- Never play until the group ahead is out of range
- Call "Fore!" immediately when a ball may endanger anyone
- Be aware of practice swing paths near other people

**Consideration:**
- Allow faster groups to play through when falling behind
- Keep phones silent or off during play
- Keep carts on designated paths and away from greens and tees

## Mental Performance Relevance

Etiquette breaches by playing partners or opponents are common Red Head triggers. A phone ringing during a backswing, an opponent walking in your line, or slow play from the group ahead — these generate frustration that has nothing to do with your own game.

Blue Head response: acknowledge the frustration, refocus on your own process. You cannot control others' behaviour. You can control your response to it. This is a core FLO principle — control the controllable.

*Source: R&A/USGA Rules of Golf 2023, Section 1; R&A etiquette guidelines*
