# Anti-Doping in Golf

## WADA Framework

Golf's anti-doping program operates under the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) Code. The International Golf Federation (IGF) is the signatory responsible for implementing the Code at international level, including Olympic competition and World Amateur Team Championships.

Professional tours implement their own anti-doping policies aligned with WADA principles:
- **PGA Tour:** Independent anti-doping program with testing and sanctions since 2008
- **DP World Tour (formerly European Tour):** Anti-doping program aligned with WADA Code
- **LPGA Tour:** Anti-doping testing program in place since 2008

## What Athletes Need to Know

All golfers competing at international amateur level or professional tour level may be subject to in-competition and out-of-competition testing. Key points:

- The WADA Prohibited List is updated annually (see wada-ama.org)
- Therapeutic Use Exemptions (TUEs) are available for legitimate medical needs
- Beta-blockers, which reduce heart rate and tremor, are prohibited in competition in golf — a significant distinction since they could affect putting and short game performance under pressure
- Common medications (cold remedies, supplements) may contain prohibited substances — always check via WADA's Global DRO (Drug Reference Online) tool

## Mental Performance Connection

Anti-doping awareness is part of professional preparation. Blue Head athletes do not leave compliance to chance — they verify medications, understand the rules, and maintain records. Red Head panic over an inadvertent violation is avoidable through simple preparation.
