# Golf History & Culture — T0 Knowledge

## Origins of Golf in Scotland

Golf originated on the eastern coast of Scotland during the 15th century. The earliest written reference is a 1457 Act of the Scottish Parliament by King James II banning golf because it distracted men from archery practice for national defence. The ban was repeated in 1471 and 1491 before being lifted after the Treaty of Glasgow in 1502.

The Old Course at St Andrews, often called the home of golf, has been played since at least the early 1400s. Its 18-hole layout became the standard — originally the course had 22 holes, reduced to 18 in 1764 when four short holes were combined into two. This arbitrary decision shaped every golf course built since.

The Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews was founded in 1754 and became the sport's governing body alongside the USGA (founded 1894). Early golf was played with leather balls stuffed with feathers (featheries), replaced by gutta-percha balls in 1848, then rubber-core Haskell balls from 1898.

Golf's mental dimension has been recognised from the start. Old Tom Morris, four-time Open Champion in the 1860s, understood that composure under pressure separated champions from talented strikers. The Red2Blue framework echoes this — the game has always demanded Blue Head clarity alongside physical skill.

## The Four Major Championships

Professional golf's four Majors are the pinnacle of competitive achievement. The Masters (Augusta National, April), the PGA Championship (rotating US venues, May), the US Open (rotating US venues, June), and The Open Championship (links courses in the UK, July) form the Grand Slam calendar.

The Open Championship is the oldest, first played at Prestwick in 1860. The US Open began in 1895, the PGA Championship in 1916, and the Masters in 1934. Only five men have won all four in their career: Gene Sarazen, Ben Hogan, Gary Player, Jack Nicklaus, and Tiger Woods.

Each Major tests different skills. The Masters rewards course knowledge and creativity on Augusta's contoured greens. The US Open demands precision under the USGA's punishing setups — narrow fairways, deep rough, firm greens. The Open challenges links golf skills — wind management, bump-and-run shots, and mental resilience across exposed terrain. The PGA Championship typically plays as a fair test of all-round excellence on strong parkland courses.

## Women's Golf — Breaking Barriers

Women's competitive golf has a rich history often overshadowed by the men's game. The Ladies' Golf Union (now merged into the R&A) was founded in 1893, making women's golf one of the earliest organised women's sports. The first Women's British Open Amateur was held that same year.

The LPGA Tour was founded in 1950 by 13 pioneering players, including Babe Zaharias, one of the greatest multi-sport athletes in history. The tour grew through the contributions of players like Mickey Wright (82 wins, widely considered the finest swing in golf history), Kathy Whitworth (88 wins, the all-time professional wins record across any gender), and Annika Sorenstam, who dominated from the late 1990s through 2008.

Women's golf has five Major championships: the Chevron Championship, the Women's PGA Championship, the US Women's Open, the Women's Open (formerly Ricoh Women's British Open), and the Evian Championship. Prize money has grown significantly but still trails the men's game.

The R&A's Women in Golf Charter, launched in 2018, committed clubs and organisations to making golf more inclusive. Participation data shows women represent roughly 24 percent of golfers worldwide, up from 19 percent in 2016.
