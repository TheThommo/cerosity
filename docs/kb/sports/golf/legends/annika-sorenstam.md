# Annika Sörenstam

**Era:** 1990s–2000s | **Role:** Professional golfer | **Nationality:** Swedish

## Bio Summary

Annika Sörenstam is the most dominant female golfer in history. She won 72 LPGA Tour events including 10 Major championships, and held the world number one ranking for 60 weeks. In 2001, she became the first woman to shoot 59 in competition, at the Standard Register PING.

Sörenstam turned professional in 1992 and won LPGA Rookie of the Year in 1994. Her peak years from 2001 to 2006 saw her win at least five times each season. In 2003, she competed in a PGA Tour event at the Colonial, becoming the first woman to play a PGA Tour event since 1945. Though she missed the cut, the courage to compete drew worldwide attention.

She retired in 2008 at age 38 to focus on family and business, returning briefly in 2021 for select events. She served as captain of the European Solheim Cup team in 2017 and has been a global ambassador for women's golf.

## Mindset Legacy

Sörenstam was known for meticulous preparation and process-driven performance. She kept detailed statistics on every aspect of her game decades before data analytics became mainstream in golf. This commitment to measurable improvement exemplifies Blue Head thinking — focusing on controllable process metrics rather than outcomes.

Her decision to play the Colonial in 2003 is a masterclass in courage under scrutiny. She managed enormous media pressure, hostile galleries, and intense self-doubt by returning to her routine. She later said the experience taught her that preparation and commitment are the only antidotes to fear.

Her pre-shot routine was among the most disciplined on any tour — consistent timing, deliberate target selection, and full commitment to every swing.
