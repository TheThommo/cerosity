# Lorena Ochoa

**Era:** 2000s | **Role:** Professional golfer | **Nationality:** Mexican

## Bio Summary

Lorena Ochoa was world number one for 158 consecutive weeks and won 27 LPGA Tour events including two Major championships before retiring at age 28 in 2010. She was the first Mexican golfer to reach number one in the world rankings in any professional tour.

Ochoa turned professional in 2002 and rose rapidly, claiming her first LPGA victory in 2004. Her peak years from 2006 to 2009 saw her dominate women's golf with a combination of power, precision, and competitive composure. She won the Women's British Open in 2007 and the Kraft Nabisco Championship in 2008.

Her decision to retire at the peak of her career to focus on family and philanthropy shocked the golf world. Ochoa founded the Lorena Ochoa Foundation, which provides education to underprivileged children in Mexico. She has since been inducted into the World Golf Hall of Fame and remains active as a golf ambassador in Latin America.
