# Phil Mickelson

**Era:** 1990s–2020s | **Role:** Professional golfer | **Nationality:** American

## Bio Summary

Phil Mickelson won six Major championships — three Masters, two PGA Championships, and one Open Championship — plus 45 PGA Tour victories. In 2021, he became the oldest Major champion in history by winning the PGA Championship at Kiawah Island at age 50.

Mickelson turned professional in 1992 after a decorated amateur career that included an NCAA individual title and a PGA Tour victory while still in college. Known as Lefty (a right-hander who plays left-handed, mirroring his father's swing), his aggressive, creative playing style made him one of golf's most popular figures for three decades.

His relationship with Major championships was defined by near-misses before breakthroughs. He finished runner-up at the US Open a record six times without winning. His first Masters victory in 2004 — after 46 previous Major starts without a win — released years of accumulated pressure and emotional weight.

## Mindset Legacy

Mickelson's career is a study in persistence through repeated failure at the highest level. Six US Open runner-up finishes would break most competitors. His ability to keep competing at Majors with full commitment despite the accumulating weight of near-misses demonstrates extraordinary mental resilience.

His aggressive style is both his greatest strength and vulnerability — a trait FLO coaching addresses through the concept of committed aggression versus reckless aggression. Mickelson at his best attacked with a plan. At his worst, he attacked impulsively. The difference was always whether Blue Head or Red Head was driving the decision.

His 2021 PGA Championship victory proved that age does not determine competitive capacity. Mickelson prepared specifically for Kiawah Island's demands, demonstrating that Blue Head strategy — adapting your game to the challenge rather than forcing the challenge to fit your game — works at any age.
