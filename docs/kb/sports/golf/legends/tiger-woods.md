# Tiger Woods

**Era:** 1990s–2020s | **Role:** Professional golfer | **Nationality:** American

## Bio Summary

Tiger Woods transformed golf from a country club pastime into a global sport. With 15 Major championships and 82 PGA Tour victories (tied for the all-time record with Sam Snead), Woods dominated the game for two decades. He held the world number one ranking for a record 683 weeks.

Woods turned professional in 1996 and won the 1997 Masters by 12 strokes at age 21 — a margin that stunned the sport. His peak from 1999 to 2002 saw him hold all four Major trophies simultaneously (the Tiger Slam). He won the 2000 US Open at Pebble Beach by 15 strokes, the largest margin in Major championship history.

After multiple knee surgeries, personal upheaval, and spinal fusion surgery in 2017, Woods completed one of sport's greatest comebacks by winning the 2019 Masters at age 43. A severe car accident in 2021 further threatened his career, yet he returned to competitive golf.

## Mindset Legacy

Woods is the ultimate case study in Blue Head under extreme pressure. His ability to close out tournaments when leading on Sunday was unmatched — his conversion rate from 54-hole leads was over 90 percent during his prime.

His pre-shot routine was robotic in its consistency. Same timing, same trigger, same commitment — regardless of whether it was a Tuesday practice round or the final hole of a Major. This routine discipline is what FLO teaches: the process does not change because the stakes change.

Woods also demonstrated that mental resilience is not the absence of setbacks but the capacity to recover from them. His 2019 Masters victory after years of doubt showed that Blue Head is a skill you can rebuild.
